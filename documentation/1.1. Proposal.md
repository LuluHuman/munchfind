# **1\. Problem Statement**

**Individual decision fatigue and Group decision conflict**. Users repeatedly experience difficulty and delay when deciding what to eat, particularly when using apps that are optimised for browsing rather than deciding. This has a real cost in time wasted, increased hunger/frustration, and a tendency to default to the same few options repeatedly rather than exploring genuinely. When groups need to agree on where to eat, existing tools offer no structured way to reach a decision. This leads to prolonged discussion, indecision, or dissatisfaction when one person's preference is chosen over others'.

The opportunity here is to build a tool that inverts the usual product logic. Instead of expanding the user's choices, *Munch Find\!* deliberately narrows the decision down to one, using randomisation constrained by user-set filters. This addresses a genuine, everyday, and currently unmet need, and does so with a mechanic simple enough to implement as a functional prototype within the scope of this module.

# **2\. Target Audience**

Students and young working adults, aged 18–30 who:

* Order food delivery or eat out **3+ times a week**  
* Regularly feel stuck or take a long time deciding what to eat  
* Are comfortable using a mobile app to make quick decisions

# **3\. Possible Solutions**

**Random single-tap decision** — removes the need to browse; one tap gives the user one store and one dish, ending the scrolling cycle entirely.

**Filtered randomisation** — lets the user narrow by budget, cuisine, or dietary restriction first, so the random pick stays relevant instead of completely blind.

**Group Mode / shared session** — multiple users join one session and receive the same randomised result, removing the need for group negotiation and eliminating the "blame" that falls on whoever chooses.

**Distance/location-based narrowing** — prioritises nearby stores so the random result is realistic and immediately actionable, not a restaurant across town.

**Re-roll with limits** *(optional/desirable)* — gives the user a small safety valve if the first result is truly unworkable, without turning the app back into a full browsing tool.

**Result history** *(optional/desirable)* — shows past picks so users can avoid repeats or revisit a place they liked, without needing to manually track it themselves.

# **3\. Expected Benefits**

**Saves time** — users reach a final food decision in under a minute instead of spending 10-20 minutes scrolling delivery apps.

**Reduces decision fatigue** — removes the mental effort of comparing endless options, especially useful after a long day or when hunger is already making decisions harder.

**Resolves group conflict** — Group Mode gives friends, coworkers, or families a neutral "decider" so no single person is blamed for a bad pick, and no time is wasted negotiating.

**Encourages food variety** — random selection nudges users toward stores or dishes they might not have picked themselves, breaking the habit of defaulting to the same 2-3 places.

**Supports dietary and budget needs** — filters ensure the randomness stays realistic and usable, not just a gimmick that ignores real constraints like allergies or spending limits.

**Low effort, low friction** — a single button press replaces multi-step browsing, comparing, and choosing, making the app genuinely faster than existing food delivery platforms for this specific use case.

**Improves user satisfaction over habitual apps** — by inverting the usual "more choice \= better" model, the app directly addresses a frustration existing platforms don't solve.
