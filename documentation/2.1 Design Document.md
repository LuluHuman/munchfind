# **1\. Project Title**

Munch Find\!

# **2\. Project Overview**

Munch Find\! is a mobile/web application that solves decision fatigue around food choices by randomly selecting a store and dish for the user, rather than presenting yet another list to scroll through. When a user can't decide what to eat, they simply open the app, apply optional filters such as budget, location, or dietary needs, and let the app choose for them. For groups, Munch Find\! offers a shared decision mode, where everyone joins a session and the app picks one option that the whole group commits to, removing the usual back-and-forth of "you choose" and the blame that follows a bad pick.

The operational need behind this project is simple: existing food delivery platforms are built to maximise browsing, not to minimise it. They show users more choices, not fewer, which actively works against people who are tired, busy, or simply can't agree with the people they're eating with. There is currently no widely-used tool that removes the choice itself as the point of value, rather than adding to it.

The expected outcome is a working prototype that demonstrates this core decision-removal mechanic, built and validated with real user input through survey and interview data. Success for this project means a functional prototype that a genuinely indecisive user, or a genuinely indecisive group, could open and use to solve the "what should we eat" problem in under a minute.

# **3\. Background and Purpose**

## **Background**

Food ordering and dining-out decisions today are handled almost entirely through general-purpose food delivery and discovery platforms, such as GrabFood, Foodpanda, and Google Maps listings. These platforms are built around browsing: they display large numbers of restaurants, menus, ratings, and photos, and expect the user to compare options and choose manually. This works well for someone who already knows what they want. It works poorly for someone who doesn't.

In practice, food decisions are frequently made in groups — friends, coworkers, or families deciding together where to eat or what to order in. Current apps have no built-in mechanism for group decision-making. The typical process falls back on group chats, verbal back-and-forth, or one person unilaterally choosing and absorbing the blame if the choice is unpopular. There is no shared, neutral, low-effort way for a group to land on a single decision.

At an individual level, users also report spending significant time scrolling through delivery apps without committing to an order, particularly when the number of options feels overwhelming. This pattern, often called decision fatigue, is a recognised behavioural effect where an abundance of choice increases the mental cost of deciding rather than reducing it.

## **Problem / Opportunity**

**Individual decision fatigue and Group decision conflict**. Users repeatedly experience difficulty and delay when deciding what to eat, particularly when using apps that are optimised for browsing rather than deciding. This has a real cost in time wasted, increased hunger/frustration, and a tendency to default to the same few options repeatedly rather than exploring genuinely. When groups need to agree on where to eat, existing tools offer no structured way to reach a decision. This leads to prolonged discussion, indecision, or dissatisfaction when one person's preference is chosen over others'.

The opportunity here is to build a tool that inverts the usual product logic. Instead of expanding the user's choices, *Munch Find\!* deliberately narrows the decision down to one, using randomisation constrained by user-set filters. This addresses a genuine, everyday, and currently unmet need, and does so with a mechanic simple enough to implement as a functional prototype within the scope of this module.

## **Purpose**

This project is being undertaken to design and build a prototype of Munch Find\!, an application whose specific purpose is to remove the burden of choice from food decisions for both individual users and groups. The operational purpose is to validate, through real user research and a working prototype, whether a decision-removal approach can meaningfully reduce the time and friction users currently experience when deciding what to eat.

# **4\. Objective**

* Deliver a working prototype of Munch Find\! that randomly selects one store and one dish for the user upon request, with no manual browsing required to reach a decision.  
* Allow users to apply at least the following filters before randomisation: budget range, food category/cuisine, and dietary restriction — so the random result is relevant, not arbitrary.  
* Provide a Group Mode where 2 or more users can join a shared session and receive one randomised result that all participants view together, removing the need for manual group negotiation.  
* Gather and incorporate real user requirements through a structured survey/interview of at least 20 respondents, ensuring the final feature set reflects actual user needs rather than assumptions.  
* Implement at least 4 mandatory requirements and at least 2 additional requirements (desirable, optional, or future enhancement) identified from the survey findings, in line with the module's minimum prototype scope.

# **5\. Target Audience / User**

Students and young working adults, aged 18–30 who:

* Order food delivery or eat out **3+ times a week**  
* Regularly feel stuck or take a long time deciding what to eat  
* Are comfortable using a mobile app to make quick decisions

# **6\. Scope**

## **6.1 Scope boundary**

In Scope:

* **A mobile/web prototype that randomly selects one food store and one dish for the user on request**  
* **Filter options (budget, cuisine/category, dietary restriction) applied before randomisation**  
* **A Group Mode allowing multiple users to join one session and receive a shared random result**  
* **Core UI screens: home/random-pick screen, filter selection, result display, group session screen**

**Out of Scope:**

* **Real payment processing or actual food ordering/checkout**  
* **Real-time integration with actual restaurant menus, pricing, or live stock/availability**  
* **Delivery tracking, rider assignment, or logistics of any kind**  
* **User account systems with persistent login, saved preferences across sessions, or social features beyond Group Mode**  
* **Native iOS/Android app store deployment (prototype only)**

## **6.2 Mandatory Functional Requirements**

**FR-001 Random Selection**  
 The system shall randomly select one store and one dish from an available dataset when the user requests a decision.

**FR-002 Filter Application**  
 The system shall allow the user to set at least one filter (budget, cuisine, or dietary restriction) before the random selection is generated, and shall apply that filter to constrain the result.

**FR-003 Result Display**  
 The system shall display the selected store name and dish clearly to the user immediately after selection, without requiring further navigation.

**FR-004 Group Mode Session**  
 The system shall allow two or more users to join a shared session and shall display the same randomly selected result to all participants in that session.

## **6.3 Mandatory Non-Functional Requirements**

**NFR-001 — Usability**  
 The system shall allow a first-time user to reach a final food decision (from opening the app to seeing a result) in under 1 minute, without needing instructions.

**NFR-002 — Performance**  
 The system shall generate and display a random result within 3 seconds of the user's request, under normal prototype testing conditions.

## **6.4 Optional or Desirable Requirements**

**OPT-001 — Re-Roll Option**  
 The system could allow the user to reject a result and request a new random selection, up to a limited number of times per session.

**OPT-002 — Location-Based Filtering**  
 The system could filter store selection based on the user's current location or a distance radius, to prioritise nearby options.

## **6.5 Possible Future Enhancement**

**FE-001 — Order History & Preference Learning**  
 Future versions could track a user's past selections and gradually weight randomisation toward cuisines the user tends to accept, rather than pure uniform randomness, improving relevance over time without removing the core "decision made for you" experience.

# **7\. Solution**

## **7.1 Solution Overview**

MunchFind is a web app that removes the decision entirely rather than trying to help users make it faster. The user opens the app, optionally sets a budget, cuisine, or dietary filter (FR-002), and taps one button. The app then randomly selects a single store and a single dish from the available dataset and shows it immediately (FR-001, FR-003). There is no browsing, no swiping through listings, and no ranked results — just one answer.

For groups, users join a shared session using a session code or QR code. Once all participants have joined, one tap generates a result that is pushed to everyone in the session at the same time (FR-004). Because the choice comes from the app and not from a person in the group, no one has to "own" the decision if the meal turns out to be a letdown.

## **7.2 Main Components**

| Feature | Description |
| :---- | :---- |
| One-Tap Decision | A single "Decide For Me" button triggers the random selection engine. |
| Filters | Budget range, cuisine type, and dietary restrictions (halal, vegetarian, no pork, etc.) can be applied before the roll. |
| Group Sessions | Multiple users join via a shareable code; everyone sees the identical result at the same time. |
| Re-Roll (limited) | Users can reject a result and reroll up to 3 times per session, after which the app locks in the last result to prevent decision-avoidance loops (OPT-001). |
| Result Card | Displays store name, dish name, estimated price, and cuisine tag in a single clean screen — no further navigation required. |
